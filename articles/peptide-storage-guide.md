# Peptide Storage Guide: Lyophilized vs. Reconstituted (Canada 2026)

_How lyophilized (freeze-dried) peptides differ from reconstituted ones for storage purposes, general handling practices, and why storage conditions matter before you even reach the dosing question._

Source: https://growthguyscanada.ca/articles/peptide-storage-guide.html · Updated: August 26, 2026 · TorontoHealth — educational only, not medical advice.


> **What's the short version?** Lyophilized (freeze-dried) peptides are commonly described as
the more stable form — sealed, frozen, with desiccant. Once reconstituted with bacteriostatic
water, the commonly described practice shifts to refrigeration and a shorter usable window.
This page covers general handling conventions, not a recommendation for any specific
compound or schedule.

## Lyophilized (freeze-dried) storage

| Condition | Commonly described handling |
| --- | --- |
| Temperature | -20°C (freezer), sealed vial |
| Desiccant | Commonly included to control moisture |
| Typical described stability window | 12–24 months when kept sealed and frozen |
| Room temperature | Generally described as degrading meaningfully faster |

These are general descriptions of common practice, not a lab-verified shelf life for any
specific vial — a Certificate of Analysis reflects the batch at the time it was tested, not
a storage guarantee. See [does a peptide COA
expire](/articles/does-a-peptide-coa-expire.html) for that distinction.

## After reconstitution

Adding bacteriostatic water changes the picture: the peptide is now in solution, which is
generally described as less stable over time than the freeze-dried form. Common practice
described in the space:

- Refrigerate (roughly 2–8°C) rather than freeze once reconstituted

- Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycling, commonly described as a stress factor

- Keep away from direct light — many peptides are described as light-sensitive, which is why vials commonly ship in amber glass

- Shorter usable windows are commonly described post-reconstitution than the sealed lyophilized form

Specifics vary by compound. This page describes general conventions in the space, not a
guarantee or instruction for any particular peptide — if you're calculating concentration or
volume, the [reconstitution calculator](/reconstitution-calculator.html) does the
arithmetic on your own numbers; it doesn't tell you what to store or how long.

## FAQ

**How long do lyophilized peptides last?** Commonly described as 12–24 months sealed at -20°C with desiccant; faster degradation at room temperature. General convention, not a guarantee for a specific vial.

**What changes once reconstituted?** The peptide is in solution, generally described as less stable, commonly refrigerated rather than frozen.

**Should reconstituted peptides be frozen?** Freeze-thaw cycling is commonly described as a stress factor; refrigeration is the more commonly described practice, but specifics vary by compound.

**Does light exposure matter?** Many peptides are described as light-sensitive, hence amber vials and dark storage.

**Is this dosing advice?** No — storage and handling only.
