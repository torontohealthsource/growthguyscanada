What does "domestic shipping" actually change?

This is the single biggest structural difference between vendors serving Canada, and it has nothing to do with product quality. A package that never crosses the border never meets a customs officer. A package that does can sit in secondary inspection for days, unrefrigerated, regardless of what's inside it or how good the vendor is.

So when a vendor ships domestically within Canada, you're not buying a better molecule — you're buying a shorter, more predictable path. That's worth something concrete, and it's worth more the more temperature-sensitive the item.

What should you check before you order from either one?

  • Find the lab name on the COA for the specific product you want, not for the vendor in general. Vendors often use different labs for different lines.
  • Verify the report on the lab's own domain. A portal that resolves a real accession number is meaningful; a link back to the vendor's own site is not.
  • Read the client field on the COA. If it names an overseas trading company rather than the vendor, you're reading a raw-material certificate.
  • Count your reversible payment options. If every option is irreversible, you carry all the risk.
  • Match the lot number on the arriving vial to the lot on the COA. This is the check that closes the loop, and almost nobody does it.
