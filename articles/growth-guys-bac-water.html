What it is, what it costs, and why it is a separate line on the order

Bacteriostatic water is sterile water carrying a small amount of preservative, used to reconstitute lyophilized compounds. The preservative is the entire point of the category: it is what allows a vial to be entered more than once rather than being a single-use diluent. At Growth Guys it is a 30 mL vial of Pfizer product at $25.00 CAD, sold separately from everything else.

Separately is not an upsell quirk. Every HGH and peptide in the catalogue ships lyophilized — a dried powder — and arrives unusable without a diluent. If you order compounds and no bacteriostatic water, you have ordered something you cannot put into solution. It is the one item people leave out of a first order and then wait another delivery cycle for.

The reconstitution arithmetic, using the published kit figures

The volume you add determines concentration, and the concentration determines what a given measurement on a syringe represents. The vendor publishes its own worked example for growth hormone: a vial reconstituted with 1.1 mL of bacteriostatic water yields a solution at 10 iu per mL, because those kit vials each contain 11 iu.

Scale from that rather than from habit. A 30 mL vial of diluent covers a great many single reconstitutions at that volume, which is why one bottle usually accompanies a multi-item order rather than one per compound. Work out your own figure from the stated vial content and the volume you intend to add, and write it down — the number that matters later is the concentration, not the volume you happened to use.

Storage, and the one product in the catalogue with no lab report

Bacteriostatic water is stored at room temperature, away from direct light, and does not require refrigeration. That is the opposite of the compounds it reconstitutes, which are refrigerated after reconstitution — so the two halves of the same order have different storage answers, and assuming one covers both is a common error.

It is also the single exception to the testing policy. The stated rule is that a product that is not lab tested is not sold, with pharmaceutical bacteriostatic water produced by Pfizer named as the one item outside it. That is a coherent exception rather than a gap: the argument for third-party assay is strongest where the manufacturer is unverifiable, and weakest for a pharmaceutical product from a named manufacturer. The vendor separately warns against off-brand or informally sourced bacteriostatic water, which is the same reasoning pointed the other way.

Working out how much you actually need

Quantity is set by how many vials you will reconstitute and at what volume, not by how many products you bought. Using the published growth hormone example of 1.1 mL per vial, a single 30 mL bottle covers a great many reconstitutions, which is why one bottle typically accompanies a multi-item order rather than one per compound.

Two things pull the other way. The preservative is what permits repeated entry of the same vial, but every entry is still an opportunity for contamination, so the number of entries is worth minimising rather than maximising. And a 30 mL bottle is $25.00 against compounds costing several times that, so running short to save a line item is a poor trade — particularly when the shortfall arrives as another delivery cycle rather than as an inconvenience.
