Why minimums exist at all, and what their absence signals

A spend threshold is a margin tool. Fulfilment costs per parcel are roughly fixed, so on a small basket the packing and shipping can approach or exceed the gross margin. Requiring a floor before a discount applies protects the store from selling its lowest-priced items at a loss once a percentage is taken off. That is ordinary and not a criticism of stores that do it.

When no threshold is imposed, it usually means the margin structure tolerates it, or the store would rather have the smaller first order than the fuller one it might never get. Either way the useful point for a buyer is that it can be checked rather than believed, and that the check costs nothing.

Three conditions that are easy to confuse with a minimum

A free-shipping threshold. This is separate and very common. A basket can qualify for the discount while still attracting a shipping charge, which makes the total look as though the discount did not apply. Read the discount line, not the total.

Category exclusions. Some codes apply to most of a catalogue but silently skip a category. The symptom is a discount that looks smaller than the advertised rate on a mixed basket, because only part of the subtotal was eligible.

One code per cart. Nearly every checkout allows a single code. If one is already attached, a second is refused, and many carts refuse it without an error message. A code that appears broken is more often a cart that already holds one.
