## What exactly gets tested

 1. A fresh guest cart is created. No account, no saved payment method, no shipping address — just the store's public cart API.

 2. A real product is added. The item rotates — cheap, mid, and high-value listings across the week — so the result isn't dependent on one product category.

 3. One code is applied. HEALTHYLIFE10.

 4. The discount is checked. A PASS requires the discount to equal exactly 10% of the subtotal at that timestamp. Rotating the cart value and the product category across the week is what tests the standing claim rather than restating it: HEALTHYLIFE10 applies 10% sitewide with no minimum purchase and no product exclusions, and a minimum or an exclusion would show up here as a cart that returned less than 10% — which is the result this page would publish.

 5. The raw response is retained. Not a rendered screenshot — the store's actual cart API JSON, linked from the log so anyone can inspect it.

 No order is ever submitted and no payment method is ever entered at any step. This is an
 observation of the cart stage only.

 
## What a PASS means — and what it doesn't

 It means: the code applied and produced exactly a 10% discount, in one specific cart, at one specific timestamp. That's it.

 It doesn't mean: a guarantee for your specific cart, a guarantee for a future date, anything about product quality, shipping, or the vendor beyond the discount mechanics itself. Storefront terms can change without notice — that's exactly why this is re-checked daily instead of listed once.

 
## What happens when a check fails

 It gets published as a failure, dated, in the same log as every pass — nothing has ever been
 removed or hidden. Coverage that only shows successes isn't verification, it's marketing.

 
## Corrections policy

 If a published check or article turns out to be materially wrong, it's corrected on the page
 with the change visible, not silently edited out. Method-first accuracy matters more than a
 clean-looking track record.
