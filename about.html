What we cover

How to read a Certificate of Analysis, how to spot a captive or fake testing lab, how COA reuse and theft work, and the practical realities of sourcing peptides domestically in Canada. What happens when a test fails

The date stops moving. It is the date of the last pass, not the date of the last attempt, so a code that stopped working freezes the stamp rather than quietly refreshing it. Failed runs are kept in the verification log next to the passes — a log containing only successes would be a marketing page, and would tell a reader nothing about whether to trust the next entry. Nothing is ordered at any point and no payment details are ever entered; every figure is read at the guest-cart stage, which is a screen any reader can reach without spending anything.

What this site will not do

It does not give dosing, protocols, or any statement about what a compound does in a body — those questions belong with a clinician, not with a page about discount codes and certificates of analysis. It does not accuse a named company of anything; where a guide describes a bad practice it describes the pattern and leaves the reader to apply it. It does not publish a purity figure, a report count or a cart result that was not read from the source on the day it is stamped. And where a figure here turns out to be wrong, it is corrected on the page rather than left standing with a newer date on top of it.
