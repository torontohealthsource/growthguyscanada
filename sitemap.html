Every page on this site, by the question it answers

This is every page on this site, filed under the question it answers rather than by date or alphabet. The sections below are ordered the way the questions usually arrive: what the code is, whether the vendor is worth buying from, what an order actually costs from Canada, and how to check any of it without taking our word for it.

Three conventions run through all of it, and they are worth knowing before you pick a page. Every figure carries the date it was checked, because a claim about a discount that is not attached to a moment cannot be wrong and therefore cannot be useful. Every laboratory result resolves on the testing laboratory's own domain rather than as a file we host, because a certificate served by the party with an interest in it is a picture of evidence rather than evidence. And this site earns a commission when the code is used, which is disclosed on every page that mentions it, because you cannot weigh a recommendation without knowing the incentive behind it.

Whether the vendor holds up

A working discount at a seller you cannot verify is a cheaper route to an unverified product, which is the least useful saving available. These pages deal with the harder question: what is published about the vendor, how much of it can be confirmed by someone outside the company, and what the published record does not establish.

What they settle: how many third-party reports exist, what the purity distribution actually looks like when every result is counted including the weak ones, and whether a given batch can be matched to a document on the laboratory's own system. What they do not settle: whether any individual vial is what its label says — we have not assayed anything ourselves, and any page claiming otherwise on our behalf is overstating it.
