# The top of the page carries most of what can be checked

Who issued it, what sample it describes, when it was analysed, and against what reference.

## Four fields decide whether the rest matters

Who issued it, what sample it describes, when it was analysed, and against what reference. A certificate missing any of the four cannot be tied to a product, a time, or a standard, and the figures below it are then unanchored regardless of how precise they look.

## Issued-to is not the same as tested-for

A report names the party who submitted the sample. That party is not necessarily the seller presenting it. A certificate issued to someone else, circulating as evidence for a different seller's product, is a common and entirely checkable situation: the name is printed on the document.

## Date of analysis versus date of issue

These are often days or weeks apart and only one of them tells you when the material was examined. A document quoting only the later date reads as more current than it is.

## The reference standard is the silent assumption

A purity figure is a comparison. The thing it was compared against determines what the number means, and a certificate that does not name it has reported a result without its unit. Look for it before reading any percentage.

## Method and instrument belong in the header too

Which analysis was run determines what the result can mean. A header naming the method lets a reader judge whether the question they care about was actually asked; one that omits it leaves the figures ambiguous in a way no amount of precision fixes.

## Check the pages match each other

Multi-page reports are frequently circulated with pages from different documents. Confirm the sample reference and the date are identical on every page before reading any result.

## A scan is a photograph of a claim

An image of a certificate establishes that an image exists. The verification route on the issuing laboratory's own site is what converts it into evidence, and most certificates circulating in this category have never been through that step.

## The header carries the identifiers

Batch or lot number, test date, sample description and the issuing laboratory are what connect a document to a physical item. A certificate missing any of them cannot be matched to what arrived, whatever its results say.

## Check the laboratory name against the portal

Where a laboratory operates its own verification portal, the header should name it and the result should resolve there. A document naming a laboratory with no independently reachable record is the weaker form of the same claim.
