# Why the origin of a parcel is a practical question, not a patriotic one

A parcel crossing a border enters a process that can hold, inspect, levy or return it, and none of those outcomes is predictable from the outside.

## Customs is the variable, not distance

A parcel crossing a border enters a process that can hold, inspect, levy or return it, and none of those outcomes is predictable from the outside. A domestic parcel skips that process entirely. The practical difference is not speed in the ordinary case; it is the width of the distribution of outcomes.

## Who carries the cost when something is held

This is the question most sellers answer vaguely. A clear policy states what happens to a held or returned parcel: replaced, refunded, or the buyer's loss. A seller shipping domestically faces this rarely enough that the policy is cheap to offer, which is part of why the answer is worth asking for.

## Stated origin and actual origin

A site can describe itself as domestic while dispatching from elsewhere. The check is the tracking number on a real order: where it first scans. That is observable after the fact and nowhere else, which is why it is worth noting on a first order rather than assuming.

## Discreet packaging is a separate question

Packaging practice and shipping origin are unrelated, and sellers often answer one when asked about the other. If the question matters to you, ask it in its own sentence and note which question the answer addressed.

## Delivery estimates are predictions, not commitments

An estimate is a statement about a typical case. The useful question is what the seller does in the untypical case, which is a policy question rather than a logistics one.

## What tracking does and does not establish

Tracking shows a parcel moving through a network. It does not establish what is inside, and a complete tracking history is sometimes presented as though it settled a dispute about contents. It settles custody, which is a narrower and still useful thing.

## What it does not tell you

Domestic shipping says nothing about what is in the parcel. It narrows a logistics risk and leaves every question about the material itself exactly where it was.

## It removes a failure mode rather than reducing one

An order that does not cross a border is never assessed for admissibility. That eliminates an outcome with no remedy, which is categorically different from making a bad outcome less likely.

## Transit time affects condition, not just patience

Shorter transit means less exposure to heat and handling for anything sensitive to either. The speed argument and the condition argument are separate, and the second is the one that affects what arrives.
