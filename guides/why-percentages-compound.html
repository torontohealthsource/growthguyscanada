Two reductions do not add, and the difference is real money

Two percentage discounts on one order do not add. The second is taken from what the first left, so 10% and 20% come to 28%, not 30%.

The arithmetic

When two percentage reductions apply to one order, the second is calculated against what the first left behind, not against the original price. Ten percent and twenty percent do not make thirty. They make twenty-eight, because the twenty is taken from ninety.

Worked on a real cart

On the cart we read for this: $365.50 CAD subtotal. The code removes $36.55 CAD. The payment-method reduction then removes $65.79 CAD — calculated against the remainder, not the original — for $102.34 CAD off in total and $263.16 CAD to pay. That is 28%, not 30%.

We published this wrong

For three days in September this site stated the combined figure as the sum of the two rates. It was wrong, it overstated the saving, and it is logged with its dates on the corrections page. A page that explains compounding while having once got it wrong should say so, because the correction is the evidence that the corrections process is real.

Where else it applies

The same arithmetic governs a code stacked on a storewide sale. The sale price becomes the base, and the code comes off that — so the combined figure is always below the sum of the two rates, on every cart, without exception.
