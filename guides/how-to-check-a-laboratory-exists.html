Start with the laboratory, not the seller

A report names an organisation. That organisation either has an independent presence -- its own site, its own published method list, its own contact route -- or it does not. Searching the name alone, without the seller's name attached, is the fastest check available, and a laboratory that appears only in one seller's documents has told you something.

Look for a verification route the lab controls

Serious laboratories publish a way to confirm a report they issued, usually a reference code checked on their own domain. A report that can only be confirmed by the party who benefits from it is not independent in the sense the word is doing work here.

Accreditation is a specific claim with a specific register

Where a laboratory claims accreditation, the accrediting body publishes a register, and the laboratory either appears on it or does not. This is a yes-or-no check that takes under a minute and is skipped almost universally. Note that accreditation covers named methods, not the organisation generally.

Check the method list against the report

A laboratory publishes what it is equipped to run. If a report claims an analysis the laboratory does not list, one of the two documents is wrong. This catches fabricated reports more reliably than examining the figures, which are easy to make plausible.

One laboratory across many sellers is normal

Finding that several unrelated sellers use the same laboratory is reassuring rather than suspicious -- it means the laboratory has a business independent of any one of them. The inverse is the warning sign.

Absence of evidence is not proof of fraud

A small laboratory may have a thin web presence and still be entirely legitimate. The checks above raise or lower confidence; none of them settles it. Treat a failed check as a reason to ask a direct question, not as a verdict.

Write down what you checked

The value of these checks compounds only if the result is recorded with a date. A note saying the register was confirmed on a given day can be re-read later; a recollection that it seemed fine cannot.
