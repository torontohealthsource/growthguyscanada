A mitochondrial-derived peptide from a research area barely a decade old, where almost everything is cell and animal work

A genuinely new class

Mitochondrial-derived peptides were only described in the last fifteen years. That makes this one of the newest subjects in this index, and newness has a specific consequence: there has not been time for independent replication, long-term observation or human trials at scale.

Most of the literature is cellular

Published work is dominated by cell-culture and rodent studies examining metabolic signalling. Those establish mechanism, which is a real contribution and a different thing from establishing an outcome in people.

Mechanism is not effect

A compound that demonstrably acts on a pathway may still produce no measurable benefit, because pathways are redundant and compensating. The step from mechanism to outcome is where most promising molecules stop, and for this one that step has not been attempted at any scale.

Why the purity figure is worth having anyway

For a compound this new, supply chains are short and quality is uneven. Confirming the material is what it claims to be removes one variable from a question that already has many.

What would move it

Independent replication outside the original research groups, then a registered human trial. Neither has happened; stating that plainly is more useful than counting papers.

The published record for this item

The published set we counted contains 8 report(s) naming MOTS-c, averaging 99.39% purity. Each resolves on the testing laboratory's own portal rather than as a file hosted by the seller.

Mechanistic plausibility is the weakest support

A proposed mechanism explains how an effect could occur. It is not evidence that the effect occurs, and mechanisms can almost always be constructed after the fact for any observed pattern.

What the human record contains

Where human data are limited to small or observational work, the honest summary names that rather than describing the compound as studied. The number of papers and the strength of evidence are different quantities.
