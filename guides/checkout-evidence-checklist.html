Checking a code yourself, in about two minutes

You can check any discount code in about two minutes without buying anything: build a cart, enter the code at the cart stage, and watch whether a discount line appears.

The check

Build a cart. Enter the code at the cart stage. Watch for the discount line. Clear the cart. That is the whole test, it costs nothing, and it is the only evidence that does not require trusting a page — including this one.

Reading the result

A line that names the code and an amount is a pass. No line is a fail. An error naming a minimum or an excluded product is a restriction, not a dead code. An error about an existing coupon means a code is already attached.

What makes a published claim checkable

Four things: it is dated to the moment of the test, the basket changes between tests, failures are published alongside passes, and the series cannot be quietly rewritten afterwards. A claim missing any of these cannot be disproved, and a claim that cannot be disproved is not evidence.

What this does not tell you

Whether the vendor is one you should buy from. A working code and a trustworthy seller are different questions, and this page only answers the first.

What the check cannot tell you

Everything in the list above stops at the cart. It says nothing about whether the order ships, how long it takes, what happens at the border, or what the seller does if something goes wrong. Those are answered by other people's experience over time, not by a cart test, and a page that implies otherwise is overselling what it measured. Treat a working code as one checkable fact among several, not as a verdict on a vendor.

What a screenshot does and does not prove

A screenshot of a working cart proves that a cart looked like that when the image was made. It does not carry a date you can trust, it cannot be re-run, and it is trivially editable by anyone with a browser's developer tools. This is not a hypothetical objection: an image is the standard evidence format in this category precisely because it is the easiest to produce. Treat a screenshot as an illustration of a claim rather than the claim's support, and ask what sits behind it.

The thirty-second version

If you do nothing else: open the store, add any item, enter the code at the cart, and look for a separate discount line equal to the stated percentage of the subtotal. That single check settles whether the code works today, which is the only tense that matters to you. Everything else on this page is about judging someone else's claim when you cannot be bothered to run your own cart — useful, but strictly second best to thirty seconds and your own browser.

What to keep, if you keep anything

If you want a record of your own, keep the cart total and the date rather than an image. Two numbers and a date are the whole claim, they are trivially comparable against a later check, and they do not invite the false confidence a screenshot carries. If you are reading someone else's record, the same test applies in reverse: a published cart figure with a date can be checked against your own cart today, which is the only way an outside claim becomes useful to you rather than merely reassuring.
