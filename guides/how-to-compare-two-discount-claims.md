# Deciding between two pages that say different things

Updated October 7, 2026.

## Compare the evidence, not the number

Faced with two pages claiming different reductions, the instinct is to take the larger. The larger number is the one more likely to be wrong, for the ordinary reason that nothing corrects an overstatement. The useful comparison is which page shows its working.

## Check the arithmetic against itself

A page that gives a subtotal, a reduction and a total has given you enough to check it in your head. Internal inconsistency -- figures that do not reconcile -- is the single most common defect, and it is visible without leaving the page.

## Check how two reductions were combined

Where a claim involves two reductions, the combined figure is either their sum or their compounded value, and which one is correct depends entirely on how the store computes them. A page asserting the combined figure without saying which mechanism applies has not done the work. A completed order resolves it; a cart preview may not.

## Check who is making the claim about whom

A page about a store, written by someone unconnected to it, carries different incentives from a page written by an affiliate or by the store itself. None of those positions makes a claim false, and disclosed affiliation is more informative than undisclosed neutrality. What matters is whether the position is stated, because an undisclosed one is the only kind that can mislead.

## Check the dates against each other

Two pages dated differently are not describing the same moment. Terms change, and the older page may have been right when written. Comparing a claim from last month with one from this morning as though they were rival accounts of the same fact is a category error.

## Prefer the page that says what it does not know

A page that marks the limits of its own claim -- this cart, this day, this payment method -- is making a smaller assertion than one that does not, and a smaller assertion is likelier to survive checking. Stated scope is a reasonable proxy for care when you have no other way to judge.

## When both are unverifiable, prefer neither

If neither page states a cart, a date or a mechanism, the honest conclusion is that you do not know, and the fastest resolution is to test it yourself. That takes about as long as reading a third page.
