Undated claims are unfalsifiable

A page saying a code is 'verified' with no date makes a claim that can never be shown to be false, because it never says when it was true. That is not a strong claim. It is an empty one, and the absence of the date is the tell.

What this does not tell you

That a code works right now. It tells you when it last did, which is the only thing any page can honestly claim about a figure it read in the past.

How old is too old

There is no universal answer, but there is a useful rule: a discount claim is worth about as much as the gap between its date and today. A code verified this morning is strong evidence; the same code verified in March is a historical note. The reason to prefer a dated claim is not that today's is always right — it is that you can apply that discount yourself, and an undated claim gives you nothing to apply it to.

Why undated claims survive so long

An undated claim cannot be falsified, so nothing ever forces its removal. A page saying a code is 'verified' remains technically unchallenged years after the code stopped working, because it never said when. Dated claims decay visibly and therefore get corrected; undated ones simply persist. That asymmetry is why the undated version is more common, not less — it is cheaper to maintain, and the cost of being wrong is carried by the reader rather than the publisher.

What a date does not promise

A date tells you when something was last true. It does not promise the claim still holds, and a page that implies otherwise is overreaching in the opposite direction. The honest reading of 'checked October 6' is 'this was the state on October 6, and nobody is claiming anything about today'. That is less reassuring than 'verified' and considerably more useful, because it tells you exactly how much weight the statement can carry and leaves the final check where it belongs, with you.

How to use a date you did not generate

Treat a published date as a lower bound on staleness, not as a guarantee. A claim checked yesterday is worth more than one checked in March, and both are worth less than your own cart thirty seconds from now. The practical use of someone else's date is triage: it tells you whether a page is maintained at all, and a site that dates its claims is one where a broken code eventually gets noticed. That is a statement about the publisher's process rather than about the code, and process is the thing you are actually choosing between.
