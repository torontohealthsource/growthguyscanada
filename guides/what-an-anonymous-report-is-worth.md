# Forum accounts as evidence: where they are strong and where they are not

An account with years of unrelated history is expensive to fabricate; one created the week it posted is not.

## Account age is the cheapest filter

An account with years of unrelated history is expensive to fabricate; one created the week it posted is not. This says nothing about any individual post's truth and a great deal about the cost of producing the post, which is the relevant quantity when assessing a body of them.

## Specificity that would be costly to invent

A report naming a figure, a date and an outcome can be checked against other reports. A report saying a seller is good cannot. The first kind accumulates into something; the second does not, however much of it there is.

## Volume is the easiest thing to manufacture

Producing many short favourable posts is cheap. Producing one detailed account that survives questioning is not. Weight accordingly, and be wary of concluding from a count.

## Negative reports are weighted differently

There is a straightforward commercial reason to manufacture praise and very little to manufacture a specific, checkable complaint. That asymmetry means a small number of detailed negative reports can be more informative than a large volume of positive ones.

## Look for reports that cost the author something

An account describing their own error, or a mixed experience, has no commercial shape. It is not thereby true, but it was not written to sell anything, which removes the most common reason to doubt it.

## Threads are a starting point for a check, not the check

The right use of a forum report is to generate a specific question that can be put to the seller or tested directly. Used that way it is valuable. Used as a verdict it carries far more weight than its evidence supports.

## What a thread cannot settle

Whether a product is what it claims to be. No quantity of user reports substitutes for an analysis, because the thing in question is not visible to the people reporting. Threads are good evidence about service, shipping and whether an operation exists; they are weak evidence about contents.

## Anonymity removes accountability, not necessarily truth

An unattributed account cannot be followed up or corroborated, which lowers its weight without making it false. The appropriate response is to treat it as a prompt to look for checkable evidence rather than as evidence itself.

## Patterns across independent venues matter more

A single anonymous report establishes little. The same specific complaint appearing across unconnected platforms, where coordination is implausible, is a meaningfully stronger signal and is worth investigating directly.
