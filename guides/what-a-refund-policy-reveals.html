The clause that tells you what the seller expects to go wrong

A refund policy is drafted around the problems a seller has actually had. An unusually specific exclusion is therefore informative: it describes a situation that arose often enough to be worth writing down.

Policies are written against experience

A refund policy is drafted around the problems a seller has actually had. An unusually specific exclusion is therefore informative: it describes a situation that arose often enough to be worth writing down. Reading the exclusions tells you more than reading the guarantee.

Who decides whether the condition was met

Most disputes turn on a judgement, not a fact. A policy reserving that judgement entirely to the seller is a guarantee only in the cases where the seller agrees. The question to look for is whether any external evidence -- a photograph, a tracking record, a test -- is accepted as settling it.

Time limits that start before you can check

A window beginning at dispatch rather than delivery can expire before a parcel arrives. This is common, usually unintentional, and visible in one sentence of the policy.

Read it before ordering, not after

A policy is a document the seller can change. The version governing an order is the one in force when it was placed, and the only reliable way to hold a copy of that version is to have read and saved it at the time.

Chargebacks are not a substitute

A payment dispute is a blunt instrument with its own time limits and evidence requirements, and some payment methods offer no route at all. A weak policy is not rescued by assuming the payment network will cover it, and which method was used changes that materially.

No policy is also an answer

A seller with no stated policy has not declined to have one; they have left it undefined, which means it is decided case by case after the money has moved.

The useful test is a small first order

Everything above is inference from a document. One small order, with the parcel tracked and the paperwork kept, converts all of it into observation at a cost known in advance.

Who pays return shipping is the test

A policy offering refunds while placing return cost on the buyer offers materially less than it appears, particularly for heavy or temperature-controlled items. The cost allocation is usually the least prominent clause.

Silence on a category is a decision

Policies that exclude something quietly, and apply the exclusion only when a claim arises, are worse than policies that state the exclusion openly. Reading for what is absent is the more informative exercise.

The window starts somewhere specific

A thirty-day policy running from dispatch rather than delivery is materially shorter than it sounds, and the difference grows with transit time. Which event starts the clock is the single most consequential detail in most policies and is rarely in the headline.
